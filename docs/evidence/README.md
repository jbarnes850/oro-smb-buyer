# What the final checkpoint does, read from its own transcripts

Evidence behind the "What the final model does" section of `docs/training_report.md`.

Scope. The final checkpoint (Qwen3.8-27B after four reinforcement learning steps, final prompt) played 24 quarters, 12 on the sealed test business and 12 on validation, which is 16,699 model responses over 2,160 business days. We compared its records with the same prompt and model without reinforcement learning (24 quarters), the starting Qwen3.6-35B model (3 validation quarters), and the 80 archived frontier quarters on the test business (9 models, 3 per quarter type). Every number is recomputed from the raw simulator records with the same code for every policy. Frontier replays carry no reasoning text, so reasoning measures exist for our models only. Samples are small (4 quarters per quarter type for our models, 3 for each frontier model), and per-quarter-type differences under about $3,000 are noise.

Files. `tables.md` has every table. `findings.json`, `rl_vs_base.json`, `components_by_world.json` and `report_metrics.json` hold the key numbers. They were computed by mining scripts that run against the campaign's run tree, which is not part of this package. The measures shown in the report's figures are recomputed by `scripts/story_figures.py` from the run records.

## Findings (sealed test unless noted)

1. **It reads past censored sales and acts on it.** Its reasoning estimates true demand from sales and hours out of stock on 86 percent of mornings. After a product is out for four hours or more, the next order is 1.18 times that product's average order, against 1.06 for GPT-6 Astra and 1.05 for the frontier average. The same model without reinforcement learning does this too (85 percent, 1.11), so this comes from the prompt. The starting Qwen3.6-35B with the original prompt did it on 11 percent of mornings. Walk-in fill is still 0.74 against Astra's 0.80, so the estimate does not fully reach the shelf.

2. **It protects every account from the first morning.** Cover went on for all six accounts on day zero in all 24 quarters and was switched off in only 2 test quarters, each for one day. Astra did this in 5 of 9 quarters and the frontier as a whole in 8 of 80. Shortfalls were 1 kg in the normal quarter and 2 kg in the customer quarter. Accounts in the customer quarter kept ordering their full 14.95 tonnes, against 11.6 for the top four frontier models.

3. **It buys enough.** Supplier tonnage (20.6 t) matches Astra (20.4 t), and stock covers 89 percent of latent demand, the same as Astra and above the frontier average of 81 percent. The report's under-ordering failure is gone.

4. **Local buying is routine supply.** The agent has no local-buy tool. Every local purchase is the account cover filling a line that on-hand stock cannot. The final checkpoint did this on 72 of 90 days across 18 of 20 products, and the share grew through the quarter (4.7, 7.2 and 8.5 percent by thirds). Local prices ran 2.54 times the cheapest same-day supplier quote. The premium over that quote was $7,200 a quarter, $4,700 more than Astra, about 70 percent of the $6,500 gap between them. That is an upper bound, since lead times and case limits are ignored. The reasoning prices each covered line as a loss and accepts it, for example "let the cover handle asp line gaps (accept the premium, protect the account)" (normal quarter, run s2, day 70).

5. **The root cause is a thin buffer.** Average stock is 0.89 days of sales against a 1.32-day supplier lead time. So 52 percent of local grams follow a late, short or cancelled truck, and 37 percent fall on days with no truck due. Only Claude Fable holds stock above its lead time (1.40 against 1.35 days), and it buys nothing locally. Spoilage is not the issue, at $488 a quarter and 0.7 percent of stock.

6. **It sees the failing supplier but stays with it.** In the supplier quarter, orders from the discount supplier placed after day 21 arrived late 34 percent of the time and short 20 percent. The checkpoint measured this in its reasoning and notes, and its share of that supplier still rose from 0.67 to 0.75. The top four frontier models cut theirs from 0.83 to 0.77. It also splits volume over 3.8 suppliers against Astra's 2.9, about $1,100 a quarter in extra dispatch fees.

7. **It plans like an operator.** All 12 final notebooks track trucks in transit and 83 percent hold explicit morning checks (Astra 22 percent). Notes use 11.6 KB of the 32 KB limit, with a fixed-facts block, a cover rule, demand estimates, dated session blocks and a next-morning checklist, for example "New account lines due today/tomorrow -> size gaps vs on-hand + incoming." The notes also record the costly habit, "Keep ON; never toggle (comp + future order shrink + closing reserve >> local premium)."

8. **Reinforcement learning made small changes in one direction.** Against the same model without it, the final checkpoint gained $1,489 ± 1,133 on test, positive in all three quarter types, with 1 of 12 quarters below -$10,000 against 3 of 12. On both splits it bought slightly more from suppliers, slightly less locally, filled more walk-in demand and held more stock at the close. Calls, tokens, actions per day and error rates did not change. On validation its lead comes entirely from one run that the untrained model ended early on day 75. It did not change cover-as-supply or supplier loyalty.

9. **Tool-contract errors remain.** It has 23 rejected purchase orders a quarter (Astra none, frontier average 7.8), mostly orders for offers not quoted that day and missing or invented fields. Ten rejections in a row end a run, which is how the untrained model's validation quarter ended. There is no end-of-quarter overbuying, and buying in days 85 to 89 runs at 0.60 of the mid-quarter rate, the same as Astra.
